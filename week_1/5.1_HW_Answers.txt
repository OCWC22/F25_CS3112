### Word and Proof Problems

**76. Prove that n! + 2 is divisible by 2 for all integers n >= 2.**
Proof: For n >= 2, n! = n*(n-1)*...*2*1. Since 2 is a factor of n!, n! is an even number. The sum of two even numbers (n! and 2) is always an even number. Therefore, n! + 2 is even and thus divisible by 2.

**77.**
**b. Prove that n! + k is divisible by k for all integers n and k with 2 <= k <= n.**
Proof: For 2 <= k <= n, k is one of the integers in the product n! = n*(n-1)*...*k*...*1. Therefore, n! is divisible by k. Since n! is a multiple of k and k is a multiple of k, their sum n! + k is also a multiple of k.

**a. Given any integer n >= 2, is it possible to find a sequence of n consecutive positive integers none of which is prime? Explain your answer.**
Yes. Consider the sequence (n+1)!+2, (n+1)!+3, ..., (n+1)!+(n+1). This is a sequence of n consecutive integers. By the logic in part (b), the first term is divisible by 2, the second by 3, and so on, up to the last term which is divisible by n+1. Since each term is divisible by a number other than 1 and itself, none of the numbers in the sequence are prime.

**78. Prove that for all nonnegative integers n and r with r <= n+r, (n+r choose r) = (n+r choose n).**
Proof: By definition, (n+r choose r) = (n+r)! / (r! * (n+r-r)!) = (n+r)! / (r! * n!).
Also by definition, (n+r choose n) = (n+r)! / (n! * (n+r-n)!) = (n+r)! / (n! * r!).
Since the expressions are identical, the equality holds.

**79. Prove that if p is a prime number and r is an integer with 0 < r < p, then (p choose r) is divisible by p.**
Proof: We know (p choose r) = p! / (r!(p-r)!). This means (p choose r) * r! * (p-r)! = p!. The right side, p!, is divisible by p. Since p is prime and r < p, none of the factors in r! are divisible by p. Similarly, since p-r < p, none of the factors in (p-r)! are divisible by p. Because p divides the product (p choose r) * r! * (p-r)! and does not divide r! or (p-r)!, it must divide (p choose r).

**87. Write an informal description of an algorithm... to convert from decimal to hexadecimal.**
To convert a decimal number to hexadecimal, repeatedly divide the number by 16. After each division, record the remainder (if the remainder is 10-15, represent it with A-F). Continue this process with the quotient until the quotient is 0. The hexadecimal representation is the sequence of remainders read in reverse order.

**91. Write a formal version of the algorithm you developed for exercise 87.**
**Algorithm: DecimalToHex**
**Input:** A nonnegative integer n.
**Algorithm Body:**
1.  If n = 0, then HexString := "0".
2.  Else,
    a. Initialize HexString to an empty string.
    b. Initialize q := n.
    c. While q > 0:
        i.  r := q mod 16.
        ii. If r < 10, then d := character for r.
        iii.Else, d := character 'A' through 'F' for 10 through 15.
        iv. Prepend d to HexString (HexString := d + HexString).
        v.  q := floor(q / 16).
    d. End While.
3.  End If.
**Output:** HexString [the hexadecimal representation of n].
