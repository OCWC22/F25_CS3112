### **Homework 9.9 Solutions: Conditional Probability, Bayes' Formula, and Independent Events**

**Q1. Suppose P(X|Y) = 1/2 and P(Y) = 1/4. What is P(X ∩ Y)?**
*   **Principle:** The definition of conditional probability is `P(X|Y) = P(X ∩ Y) / P(Y)`.
*   **Step 1:** Rearrange the formula to solve for the intersection: `P(X ∩ Y) = P(X|Y) * P(Y)`.
*   **Step 2:** Substitute the given values: `P(X ∩ Y) = (1/2) * (1/4)`.
*   **Answer:** `P(X ∩ Y) = 1/8`.

**Q2. Suppose P(X) = 1/3, P(Y) = 1/5, and P(X ∩ Y) = 1/6. What is P(X|Y)?**
*   **Principle:** Use the formula for conditional probability.
*   **Step 1:** `P(X|Y) = P(X ∩ Y) / P(Y)`.
*   **Step 2:** Substitute the values: `P(X|Y) = (1/6) / (1/5) = (1/6) * 5`.
*   **Answer:** `P(X|Y) = 5/6`.

**Q3. 25% of students received an A on the first test, 15% on the second, and 10% on both. What is the probability that a student who received an A on the first test also received one on the second?**
*   **Principle:** Conditional Probability.
*   **Step 1:** Define events: `T1` = A on test one, `T2` = A on test two. We need `P(T2 | T1)`.
*   **Step 2:** Given: `P(T1) = 0.25`, `P(T2) = 0.15`, `P(T1 ∩ T2) = 0.10`.
*   **Step 3:** Apply the formula: `P(T2 | T1) = P(T1 ∩ T2) / P(T1) = 0.10 / 0.25`.
*   **Answer:** 0.4 or 40%.

**Q4. A condition affects 1 in 1000 people. A test has a 1% false negative rate and a 4% false positive rate. Find P(has condition | tests positive).**
*   **Principle:** Bayes' Theorem is used to find the probability of a cause given an effect.
*   **Step 1:** Define events: `C` = Has Condition, `T` = Tests Positive.
*   **Step 2:** List given probabilities:
    *   `P(C) = 1/1000 = 0.001`, so `P(C') = 1 - 0.001 = 0.999`.
    *   False Negative `P(T'|C) = 0.01`, so True Positive `P(T|C) = 1 - 0.01 = 0.99`.
    *   False Positive `P(T|C') = 0.04`.
*   **Step 3:** Write Bayes' Formula: `P(C|T) = [P(T|C)P(C)] / [P(T|C)P(C) + P(T|C')P(C')]`.
*   **Step 4:** Substitute values: `P(C|T) = (0.99 * 0.001) / [(0.99 * 0.001) + (0.04 * 0.999)]`
    `= 0.00099 / (0.00099 + 0.03996) = 0.00099 / 0.04095`.
*   **Answer:** Approximately 0.0242 or 2.42%.

**Q5. If A and B are independent, P(A)=1/3, and P(B)=1/4, what is P(A|B)?**
*   **Principle:** For independent events, the occurrence of one does not affect the probability of the other.
*   **Step 1:** By the definition of independence, `P(A|B) = P(A)`.
*   **Answer:** 1/3.

**Q6. An urn contains 25 red and 15 blue balls. Two are chosen without replacement. What is the probability the second is blue, given the first was red?**
*   **Principle:** Conditional probability based on a reduced sample space.
*   **Step 1:** After one red ball is drawn, the urn contains 24 red and 15 blue balls.
*   **Step 2:** The total number of balls remaining is `24 + 15 = 39`.
*   **Step 3:** The probability of drawing a blue ball now is the number of blue balls divided by the new total.
*   **Answer:** `15 / 39 = 5/13`.

**Q7. Urn 1 (3R, 2B), Urn 2 (1R, 4B). One ball is drawn from each.**
*   **Principle:** The draws from the two urns are independent events.
*   (a) P(both red) = `P(R from U1) * P(R from U2) = (3/5) * (1/5) = 3/25`.
*   (b) P(both blue) = `P(B from U1) * P(B from U2) = (2/5) * (4/5) = 8/25`.
*   (c) P(one red, one blue) = `P(R1, B2) + P(B1, R2) = (3/5)(4/5) + (2/5)(1/5) = 12/25 + 2/25 = 14/25`.
*   (d) P(at least one red) = `1 - P(both blue) = 1 - 8/25 = 17/25`.

**Q8. 10 semifinalists (7 men, 3 women). Two finalists are chosen.**
*   **Principle:** Dependent events, as the selections are without replacement.
*   (a) P(both men) = `P(M1) * P(M2|M1) = (7/10) * (6/9) = 42/90 = 7/15`.
*   (b) P(both women) = `P(W1) * P(W2|W1) = (3/10) * (2/9) = 6/90 = 1/15`.
*   (c) P(one man, one woman) = `1 - P(both men) - P(both women) = 1 - 7/15 - 1/15 = 7/15`.

**Q9. Prove Bayes' Theorem for n=2 mutually disjoint events.**
*   **Goal:** Prove `P(B1|A) = [P(A|B1)P(B1)] / [P(A|B1)P(B1) + P(A|B2)P(B2)]`.
*   **Step 1 (Numerator):** From the definition of conditional probability, `P(A ∩ B1) = P(A|B1)P(B1)`.
*   **Step 2 (Denominator):** Since B1 and B2 form a partition of the sample space, `A = (A ∩ B1) ∪ (A ∩ B2)`. Because B1 and B2 are disjoint, so are `(A ∩ B1)` and `(A ∩ B2)`. Thus, `P(A) = P(A ∩ B1) + P(A ∩ B2)`.
*   **Step 3:** Rewriting Step 2 using the rule from Step 1 gives `P(A) = P(A|B1)P(B1) + P(A|B2)P(B2)`.
*   **Step 4:** By definition, `P(B1|A) = P(A ∩ B1) / P(A)`.
*   **Step 5:** Substitute the expressions from Step 1 (numerator) and Step 3 (denominator) into Step 4 to yield the theorem. **Proof complete.**

**Q10. Prove the full version of Bayes' Theorem.**
*   **Principle:** The proof is a direct generalization of the case for n=2.
*   **Theorem:** For a partition of a sample space `B1, B2, ..., Bn` and an event A, `P(Bk|A) = [P(A|Bk)P(Bk)] / [Σ P(A|Bi)P(Bi)]` for `i=1 to n`.
*   **Proof Sketch:** The numerator, `P(A|Bk)P(Bk)`, is equal to `P(A ∩ Bk)`. The denominator is an expansion of `P(A)` using the Law of Total Probability over the partition `B1, ..., Bn`. The final step is substituting these into the definition `P(Bk|A) = P(A ∩ Bk) / P(A)`.

**Q13. Urn 1 (10R, 25G), Urn 2 (15R, 5G). Loaded coin P(H)=3/4 selects Urn 1. Find P(Green).**
*   **Principle:** Law of Total Probability.
*   **Step 1:** `P(G) = P(G|Urn1)P(Urn1) + P(G|Urn2)P(Urn2)`.
*   **Step 2:** `P(Urn1) = P(H) = 3/4`, `P(Urn2) = P(T) = 1/4`.
*   **Step 3:** `P(G|Urn1) = 25/35 = 5/7`, `P(G|Urn2) = 5/20 = 1/4`.
*   **Step 4:** `P(G) = (5/7)(3/4) + (1/4)(1/4) = 15/28 + 1/16 = 60/112 + 7/112`.
*   **Answer:** `67/112`.

**Q14. Drug test: 4% use drugs. Test has 98% true positive rate and 5% false positive rate. Find P(user | positive test).**
*   **Principle:** Bayes' Theorem.
*   **Step 1:** `D`=User, `T`=Positive Test. `P(D)=0.04`, `P(D')=0.96`, `P(T|D)=0.98`, `P(T|D')=0.05`.
*   **Step 2:** `P(D|T) = [P(T|D)P(D)] / [P(T|D)P(D) + P(T|D')P(D')]`.
*   **Step 3:** `P(D|T) = (0.98 * 0.04) / [(0.98 * 0.04) + (0.05 * 0.96)] = 0.0392 / (0.0392 + 0.048)`.
*   **Answer:** `0.0392 / 0.0872 ≈ 0.4495` or 45.0%.

**Q15. Two factories, 2% and 5% defective. Factory 1 supplies 180 of 260 parts.**
*   **Principle:** Conditional probability and Bayes' Theorem.
*   **Step 1:** `F1`=Fac 1, `F2`=Fac 2, `D`=Defective. `P(F1)=180/260=18/26`, `P(F2)=80/260=8/26`. `P(D|F1)=0.02`, `P(D|F2)=0.05`.
*   (a) P(defective from F1) = `P(D ∩ F1) = P(D|F1)P(F1) = 0.02 * (18/26) ≈ 0.0138`.
*   (b) P(defective from F2) = `P(D ∩ F2) = P(D|F2)P(F2) = 0.05 * (8/26) ≈ 0.0154`.
*   (c) P(defective) = `P(D) = P(D ∩ F1) + P(D ∩ F2) ≈ 0.0138 + 0.0154 = 0.0292`.
*   (d) P(from F1 | defective) = `P(F1|D) = P(D ∩ F1) / P(D) ≈ 0.0138 / 0.0292 ≈ 0.4726`.

**Q16. Three suppliers X, Y, Z with supplies 60%, 20%, 20% and defect rates 8%, 12%, 15%.**
*   **Principle:** Law of Total Probability and Bayes' Theorem.
*   (a) P(superior grade) = `1 - P(defective)`.
    *   `P(D) = P(D|X)P(X) + P(D|Y)P(Y) + P(D|Z)P(Z)`
    *   `P(D) = (0.08)(0.6) + (0.12)(0.2) + (0.15)(0.2) = 0.048 + 0.024 + 0.03 = 0.102`.
    *   `P(Superior) = 1 - 0.102 = 0.898`.
*   (b) P(from X | superior) = `P(X|S) = P(S|X)P(X) / P(S)`.
    *   `P(S|X) = 1 - 0.08 = 0.92`.
    *   `P(X|S) = (0.92 * 0.6) / 0.898 = 0.552 / 0.898 ≈ 0.6147`.

**Q18. Pair of dice. E=sum is 6, F=first die is 4. Are they independent?**

**Q20. Fair coin tossed three times. A=first is H, B=last is H. Are they independent?**
*   **Principle:** Test for independence.
*   **Step 1:** `P(A)=4/8=1/2`. `P(B)=4/8=1/2`.
*   **Step 2:** `A ∩ B` is the set {HHH, HTH}. `P(A ∩ B)=2/8=1/4`.

*   **Conclusion:** Since `P(A ∩ B) = P(A)P(B)`, the events **are independent**.

**Q21. A, B independent, A ∩ B = ∅. What must be true?**
*   **Principle:** Logic of independence and disjoint events.
*   **Step 1:** Since A and B are independent, `P(A ∩ B) = P(A)P(B)`.
*   **Step 2:** Since `A ∩ B = ∅`, `P(A ∩ B) = 0`.
*   **Step 3:** Therefore, `P(A)P(B) = 0`. This implies that either `P(A)=0` or `P(B)=0` (or both).

**Q22. Prove that if A and B are independent, then A and B' are also independent.**
*   **Principle:** Set theory and the definition of independence.
*   **Step 1:** We need to show `P(A ∩ B') = P(A)P(B')`.
*   **Step 2:** The event A can be partitioned into two disjoint sets: `(A ∩ B)` and `(A ∩ B')`. So, `P(A) = P(A ∩ B) + P(A ∩ B')`.
*   **Step 3:** Rearrange to get `P(A ∩ B') = P(A) - P(A ∩ B)`.
*   **Step 4:** Since A and B are independent, substitute `P(A ∩ B) = P(A)P(B)`. This gives `P(A ∩ B') = P(A) - P(A)P(B)`.
*   **Step 5:** Factor out P(A): `P(A ∩ B') = P(A)(1 - P(B))`.
*   **Step 6:** Since `1 - P(B) = P(B')`, we have `P(A ∩ B') = P(A)P(B')`. **Proof complete.**

**Q23. Multiple choice, 5 options, 2 questions. Student knows one answer, guesses the other.**

**Q26. Describe events A, B, C that are pairwise independent but not mutually independent.**
*   **Example:** Toss a fair coin twice.
*   **Events:**
    *   A = First toss is Heads {HH, HT}. P(A)=1/2.
    *   B = Second toss is Heads {HH, TH}. P(B)=1/2.
    *   C = Exactly one toss is Heads {HT, TH}. P(C)=1/2.
*   **Pairwise Check:**
    *   `P(A ∩ B)` {HH} = 1/4. `P(A)P(B) = (1/2)(1/2)=1/4`. (Independent)
    *   `P(A ∩ C)` {HT} = 1/4. `P(A)P(C) = (1/2)(1/2)=1/4`. (Independent)
    *   `P(B ∩ C)` {TH} = 1/4. `P(B)P(C) = (1/2)(1/2)=1/4`. (Independent)
*   **Mutual Check:**
    *   The event `A ∩ B ∩ C` is impossible (first is H, second is H, and exactly one is H). So `P(A ∩ B ∩ C) = 0`.
    *   However, `P(A)P(B)P(C) = (1/2)(1/2)(1/2) = 1/8`.
    *   Since `0 ≠ 1/8`, the events are not mutually independent.

**Q28. Loaded coin P(H)=0.7, tossed 10 times.**

*   (a) P(7 heads) = `C(10,7) * (0.7)^7 * (0.3)^3 = 120 * (0.08235) * (0.027) ≈ 0.2668`.

*   (d) P(at least one head) = `1 - P(0 heads) ≈ 1 - 0.0000059 = 0.9999941`.

**Q29. 10 items are chosen from a batch with a 3% defect rate.**

**Q32. Multiple choice, 4 options, 3 questions. Student guesses all.**
*   **Principle:** Binomial probability. `p=1/4`, `1-p=3/4`.
*   (a) P(all 3 correct) = `(1/4)^3 = 1/64`.
*   (b) P(exactly 2 correct) = `C(3,2) * (1/4)^2 * (3/4)^1 = 3 * (1/16) * (3/4) = 9/64`.
*   (c) P(exactly 1 correct) = `C(3,1) * (1/4)^1 * (3/4)^2 = 3 * (1/4) * (9/16) = 27/64`.
*   (d) P(none correct) = `(3/4)^3 = 27/64`.
*   (e) Expected number of correct answers = `n * p = 3 * (1/4) = 3/4`.
    *   Expected score = `(1 * P(1 correct)) + (0.5 * P(2 correct)) - (0.5 * P(3 correct)) - (0.5 * P(0 correct))`
    *   `= (1 * 27/64) + (0.5 * 9/64) - (0.5 * 1/64) - (0.5 * 27/64)`
    *   `= (27 + 4.5 - 0.5 - 13.5) / 64 = 17.5 / 64 ≈ 0.273`.

**Q33. Gambler problem (Gambler's Ruin).**
*   **Principle:** This problem is modeled by a recurrence relation. Let `Pk` be the probability of eventual ruin (reaching $0) starting with `$k`.
*   **Setup:**
    *   The game is defined by winning $1 with probability `p=1/6` and losing $1 with probability `q=5/6`.
    *   After one play, the gambler is in state `k+1` (with probability `p`) or state `k-1` (with probability `q`).
    *   The probability of ruin from state `k` is the weighted average of the probabilities of ruin from these subsequent states.
*   **Recurrence Relation:** `Pk = p * P(k+1) + q * P(k-1)`.
*   **Derivation:** Substituting `p=1/6` and `q=5/6`, the equation to be solved is `Pk = (1/6)P(k+1) + (5/6)P(k-1)`. This is the derivation requested by the question. Solving this requires setting up boundary conditions (e.g., `P0 = 1`, `P(goal) = 0`) and using techniques for linear difference equations.
