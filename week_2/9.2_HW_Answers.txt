## 1. World Series Outcomes (Team A wins first three)
**Problem:** In I-4, use the fact that in baseball's World Series, the first team to win four games wins the series. Suppose team A wins the first three games. How many ways can the series be completed? (Draw a tree.)

**Solution:**
If Team A has won the first three games (AAA), the series can end in the following ways:
- **A wins the 4th game:** The series ends. Outcome: AAAA.
- **B wins the 4th game, A wins the 5th:** Outcomes: AAABA.
- **B wins games 4-5, A wins the 6th:** Outcomes: AAABBA.
- **B wins games 4-6, A wins the 7th:** Outcomes: AAABBBA.

There are **4 ways** the series can be completed. The tree diagram starts with `AAA` and branches for each subsequent game winner until A wins a 4th game.

## 3. World Series Outcomes (A wins four in a row)
**Problem:** How many ways can a World Series be played if team A wins four games in a row?

**Solution:**
If Team A wins four games in a row, the series is over. There is only one way for this to happen: `AAAA`.
- **Answer:** 1 way.

## 5. Competition between X and Y
**Problem:** In a competition between players X and Y, the first player to win three games in a row or a total of four games wins. How many ways can the competition be played if X wins the first game and Y wins the second and third games?

**Solution:**
The state of the competition is `XYY`. Total wins: X=1, Y=2. Neither has won 3 in a row.
- **Game 4: Y wins.** `XYYY`. Y wins with 3 in a row. (1 way)
- **Game 4: X wins.** `XYYX`. Total wins: X=2, Y=2. No one has won 3 in a row.
  - **Game 5: X wins.** `XYYXX`. Total wins: X=3, Y=2. No one has won 3 in a row.
    - **Game 6: X wins.** `XYYXXX`. X wins with 3 in a row. (1 way)
    - **Game 6: Y wins.** `XYYXXY`. Total wins: X=3, Y=3.
      - **Game 7: X wins.** `XYYXXYX`. X wins with 4 total games. (1 way)
      - **Game 7: Y wins.** `XYYXXYY`. Y wins with 4 total games. (1 way)
  - **Game 5: Y wins.** `XYYXY`. Total wins: X=2, Y=3. No one has won 3 in a row.
    - **Game 6: Y wins.** `XYYXYY`. Y wins with 4 total games. (1 way)
    - **Game 6: X wins.** `XYYXYX`. Total wins: X=3, Y=3.
      - **Game 7: X wins.** `XYYXYXX`. X wins with 4 total games. (1 way)
      - **Game 7: Y wins.** `XYYXYXY`. Y wins with 4 total games. (1 way)

Total ways: 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 = **7 ways**.

## 7. Urn Experiment
**Problem:** One urn contains one blue ball (B1) and three red balls (R1, R2, R3). A second urn contains two red balls (R4, R5) and two blue balls (B2, B3). An experiment is performed in which one of the two urns is chosen at random and then two balls are randomly chosen from it, one after the other without replacement.
a. Construct the possibility tree showing all possible outcomes of this experiment.
b. What is the total number of outcomes of this experiment?
c. What is the probability that two red balls are chosen?

**Solution:**
a. **Possibility Tree:**
   - **Path 1: Choose Urn 1 (P=1/2)**
     - It has 4 balls (B1, R1, R2, R3). Ways to pick 2: `C(4,2) = 6`.
     - Outcomes: (B1,R1), (B1,R2), (B1,R3), (R1,R2), (R1,R3), (R2,R3).
   - **Path 2: Choose Urn 2 (P=1/2)**
     - It has 4 balls (R4, R5, B2, B3). Ways to pick 2: `C(4,2) = 6`.
     - Outcomes: (R4,R5), (R4,B2), (R4,B3), (R5,B2), (R5,B3), (B2,B3).
b. **Total Outcomes:** There are 6 outcomes from Urn 1 and 6 from Urn 2. Total = **12 outcomes**.
c. **Probability of two red balls:**
   - From Urn 1: 3 ways to pick 2 red balls: (R1,R2), (R1,R3), (R2,R3). `P(2R | Urn1) = 3/6 = 1/2`.
   - From Urn 2: 1 way to pick 2 red balls: (R4,R5). `P(2R | Urn2) = 1/6`.
   - Total Probability: `P(2R) = P(2R | Urn1)P(Urn1) + P(2R | Urn2)P(Urn2)`
   - `P(2R) = (1/2)*(1/2) + (1/6)*(1/2) = 1/4 + 1/12 = 3/12 + 1/12 = 4/12 = 1/3`.
   - **Answer:** 1/3.

## 9. Travel Routes
**Problem:** Suppose there are three roads from city A to city B and five roads from city B to city C.
a. How many ways is it possible to travel from city A to city C via city B?
b. How many different round-trip routes are there from city A to C to B and back to A?
c. How many different routes are there from A to C to B and back to A in which no road is traversed twice?

**Solution:**
a. **A to C via B:** `3 (A->B) * 5 (B->C) = 15` ways.
b. **Round-trip (A->C->A):** `15 (A->C) * 15 (C->A) = 225` ways.
c. **Round-trip with no road repeated:**
   - A to C: `3 * 5 = 15` ways.
   - C to B: 4 remaining roads.
   - B to A: 2 remaining roads.
   - Total: `3 * 5 * 4 * 2 = 120` ways.

## 11. Bit Strings
**Problem:**
a. How many bit strings have length 8?
b. How many bit strings of length 8 begin with three 0's?
c. How many bit strings of length 8 begin and end with a 1?

**Solution:**
a. **Length 8:** 2 choices for each of 8 positions. `2^8 = 256`.
b. **Begin with 000:** The first 3 bits are fixed. 5 bits remain. `1*1*1 * 2^5 = 32`.
c. **Begin and end with 1:** The first and last bits are fixed. 6 bits remain. `1 * 2^6 * 1 = 64`.

## 13. Coin Toss Outcomes
**Problem:** A coin is tossed four times. An outcome of HHTT means that heads were obtained on the first two tosses and tails on the second two. Assume that heads and tails are equally likely.
a. How many distinct outcomes are possible?
b. What is the probability that exactly two heads occur?
c. What is the probability that exactly one head occurs?

**Solution:**
a. **Distinct outcomes:** `2^4 = 16`.
b. **Exactly two heads:** Choose 2 positions for H out of 4. `C(4,2) = 6`. Probability = `6/16 = 3/8`.
c. **Exactly one head:** Choose 1 position for H out of 4. `C(4,1) = 4`. Probability = `4/16 = 1/4`.

## 15. Combination Lock
**Problem:** A combination lock requires three selections of numbers, each from 1 through 30.
a. How many different combinations are possible?
b. Suppose the locks are constructed in such a way that no number may be used twice. How many different combinations are possible?

**Solution:**
a. **Repetition allowed:** `30 * 30 * 30 = 30^3 = 27,000`.
b. **No repetition:** `P(30,3) = 30 * 29 * 28 = 24,360`.

## 17. Integers from 1000 to 9999
**Problem:**
a. How many integers are there from 1000 through 9999?
b. How many odd integers are there from 1000 through 9999?
c. How many integers from 1000 through 9999 have distinct digits?
d. How many odd integers from 1000 through 9999 have distinct digits?
e. What is the probability that a randomly chosen four-digit integer has distinct digits and is odd?

**Solution:**
a. **Total integers:** `9999 - 1000 + 1 = 9000`.
b. **Odd integers:** First digit (1-9), next two (0-9), last digit (1,3,5,7,9). `9 * 10 * 10 * 5 = 4500`.
c. **Distinct digits:** `9 (no 0) * 9 * 8 * 7 = 4536`.
d. **Odd with distinct digits:**
   - Case 1: Last digit is from {1,3,5,7,9}. First digit can't be 0 or the last digit. `8 * 8 * 7 * 5 = 2240`.
   - **Answer:** 2240.
e. **Probability:** `(Odd with distinct digits) / (Total integers) = 2240 / 9000 = 224/900 = 56/225`.

## 19. PINs
**Problem:** Three officers—a president, a treasurer, and a secretary—are to be chosen from among four people: Ann, Bob, Cyd, and Dan. Suppose that for various reasons, Bob is not qualified to be treasurer, and Cyd's other commitments make it impossible for her to be secretary. How many ways can the officers be chosen? Can the multiplication rule be used to solve this problem?

**Solution:**
Yes, the multiplication rule can be used, but it's easier to use inclusion-exclusion or count cases.
- **Total ways without restrictions:** `P(4,3) = 4*3*2 = 24`.
- **Invalid ways:**
  - Bob is treasurer: `_ B _`. 3 choices for President, 2 for Secretary. `3*1*2 = 6` ways.
  - Cyd is secretary: `_ _ C`. 3 for President, 2 for Treasurer. `3*2*1 = 6` ways.
  - Both (Bob is T, Cyd is S): `A B C` or `D B C`. 2 ways.
- **Valid ways:** `Total - (Bob=T) - (Cyd=S) + (Both) = 24 - 6 - 6 + 2 = 14` ways.

## 21. Relations and Functions
**Problem:** Suppose A is a set with m elements and B is a set with n elements.
a. How many relations are there from A to B?
b. How many functions are there from A to B?
c. What fraction of the relations from A to B are functions?

**Solution:**
a. A relation is a subset of `A x B`. `|A x B| = mn`. Number of subsets is `2^(mn)`.
b. A function maps each of `m` elements in A to one of `n` elements in B. `n * n * ... * n` (m times) = `n^m`.
c. Fraction: `(n^m) / (2^(mn))`.

## 23. Integers represented by strings
**Problem:** In Section 2.5 we showed how integers can be represented by strings of 0's and 1's inside a digital computer. One commonly used code is the Extended Binary-Coded Decimal Interchange Code (EBCDIC). How many distinct symbols can be represented by this code if each symbol has an 8-bit representation?

**Solution:**

## 31. Prime Divisors
**Problem:**
a. If p is a prime and a is a positive integer, how many distinct positive divisors does p^a have?
b. If p and q are distinct primes and a and b are positive integers, how many distinct positive divisors does p^a * q^b have?
c. If p, q, and r are distinct primes and a, b, and c are positive integers, how many distinct positive divisors does p^a * q^b * r^c have?
d. If p1, ..., pm are distinct prime numbers and a1, ..., am are positive integers, how many distinct positive divisors does (p1^a1)...(pm^am) have?
e. What is the smallest positive integer with exactly 12 divisors?

**Solution:**
a. Divisors are `p^0, p^1, ..., p^a`. There are `a+1` divisors.
b. Divisors are of the form `p^i * q^j` where `0<=i<=a` and `0<=j<=b`. There are `(a+1)(b+1)` divisors.
c. `(a+1)(b+1)(c+1)` divisors.
d. `(a1+1)(a2+1)...(am+1)` divisors.
e. We need `(a1+1)...(am+1) = 12`. We can factor 12 as 12, 6*2, 4*3, or 3*2*2.
   - `12`: `p^11`. Smallest is `2^11 = 2048`.
   - `6*2`: `p1^5 * p2^1`. Smallest is `2^5 * 3^1 = 32 * 3 = 96`.
   - `4*3`: `p1^3 * p2^2`. Smallest is `2^3 * 3^2 = 8 * 9 = 72`.
   - `3*2*2`: `p1^2 * p2^1 * p3^1`. Smallest is `2^2 * 3 * 5 = 60`.
   - **Answer:** 60.

## 33. Seating Arrangements
**Problem:** Six people attend the theater and sit in a row with exactly six seats.
a. How many ways can they be seated together in the row?
b. Suppose one of the six is a doctor who must sit on the aisle. How many ways can the people be seated in the row with the doctor in an aisle seat?
c. Suppose the six people consist of three married couples and each couple wants to sit together with the husband on the left. How many ways can the six be seated together in the row?

**Solution:**
a. `6! = 720` ways.
b. There are 2 aisle seats. The doctor sits in one (2 choices). The other 5 people can be arranged in the remaining 5 seats in `5!` ways. `2 * 5! = 2 * 120 = 240` ways.
c. Treat each couple (Husband-Wife) as a single block. There are 3 such blocks. These 3 blocks can be arranged in `3!` ways. The internal arrangement is fixed (husband on left). `3! = 6` ways.

## 35. Permutations
**Problem:**
a. Write all the 2-permutations of {W, X, Y, Z}.
b. Write all the 3-permutations of {s, t, u, v}.

**Solution:**
a. **2-permutations of {W, X, Y, Z}:**
   WX, WY, WZ, XW, XY, XZ, YW, YX, YZ, ZW, ZX, ZY. (12 total)
b. **3-permutations of {s, t, u, v}:**
   stu, sut, tsu, tus, ust, uts, ... (and so on for all combinations).
   Total is `P(4,3) = 24`.

## 37. Evaluate Quantities
**Problem:** Evaluate the following quantities.
a. P(6, 4)
b. P(6, 6)
c. P(6, 3)
d. P(6, 1)

**Solution:**
a. `P(6, 4) = 6*5*4*3 = 360`.
b. `P(6, 6) = 6! = 720`.
c. `P(6, 3) = 6*5*4 = 120`.
d. `P(6, 1) = 6`.
