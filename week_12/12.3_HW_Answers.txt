Section 12.3 Homework Answers

12.3-2 - Searches after insertion examine exactly 1 more node

When you inserted v, you followed comparisons down to the first NIL child and stopped there. A later successful search for v follows the same path, but it stops one step earlier (at v instead of NIL). Thus "search nodes examined" = ("insert nodes examined") + 1.

12.3-3 - Sort by building a BST, then inorder

• Worst-case time: Θ(n²) (inserting already sorted input creates a chain; each insert can be Θ(n))

• Best-case time: Θ(n log n) (if the tree stays balanced: total insert Θ(n log n) + inorder Θ(n))

(For guaranteed Θ(n log n) use self-balancing trees like AVL/B-trees.)

12.3-5 - Is deletion "commutative"? (delete x then y vs. y then x)

No. Counterexample (keys 4,3,7,6). Start with:

    4
   / \
  3   7
     /
    6

• Delete 4 then 3 → replacement makes root 6, then removing 3 yields:

  6
   \
    7

• Delete 3 then 4 → tree becomes

  4
   \
    7
   /
  6

then deleting 4 produces root 7 with left 6:

  7
 /
6

Final trees differ ⇒ not commutative. (Many standard write-ups use this exact example.)
