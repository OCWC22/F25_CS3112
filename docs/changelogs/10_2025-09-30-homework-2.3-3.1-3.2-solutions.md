## Reasoning

### Educational Approach
Solutions are written for **ground-zero learning** with:
1. **Concept explanations** before each problem (what is induction? recursion? binary search?)
2. **Step-by-step derivations** showing all algebraic manipulations
3. **Intuitive explanations** alongside formal proofs
4. **Concrete examples** with traces and tables
5. **Visual formatting** with clear sections and subsections

### Pedagogical Structure
Each solution follows:
- **Problem statement** with context
- **Background concepts** (definitions, prerequisites)
- **Detailed solution** with numbered steps
- **Formal proof** with mathematical rigor
- **Intuitive explanation** for understanding
- **Examples/verification** where applicable
- **Summary/conclusion** highlighting key takeaways

### Mathematical Rigor
All proofs include:
- Formal definitions of asymptotic notation
- Proper use of quantifiers (∃, ∀)
- Clear statement of assumptions
- Logical flow from premises to conclusion

- Both directions for "if and only if" proofs

## Notes

### Key Insights Highlighted
1. **Binary search doesn't improve insertion sort** because data movement (Θ(n²)) dominates comparison reduction (Θ(n lg n))
2. **Selection sort is non-adaptive** - always Θ(n²) regardless of input
3. **Asymptotic notation misuse** - "at least O(n²)" is meaningless
4. **Exponential growth differences** - constant factors in exponent matter (2^(2n) ≠ O(2^n))

### Teaching Philosophy
Solutions emphasize **why** over **what** - explaining the reasoning behind each step rather than just stating results. This approach supports both learning and demonstrating understanding to professors.
